Dramatis Personae

Every mark tells a story

Nine mortals and gods carry the saga. Each is given a seal here in place of a portrait — a symbol of the thing that defines them.

King of Ithaca

Odysseus

Clever, battle-tested, and slowly hardening. He must out-think gods and monsters alike to bring his crew home — even as the cost changes who he is.

Queen of Ithaca

Penelope

Twenty years of waiting, and twenty years of outwitting men who won't take no for an answer. Her patience is its own kind of warfare.

Prince of Ithaca

Telemachus

A boy raised on his father's absence, learning — song by song — what it costs to become the man Ithaca needs him to be.

Goddess of Wisdom

Athena

Odysseus' divine protector, equal parts mentor and strategist — and the one who sees furthest ahead when he cannot.

God of the Sea

Poseidon

Blinded pride made divine. He holds the whole ocean against one man's ship, and he is in no hurry to forgive.

First of the Suitors

Antinous

The loudest voice at Penelope's table and the most certain of his own victory — first to raise a hand against Telemachus, first to fall.

King of the Gods

Zeus

Balance made throne. He steps into mortal affairs only enough to keep the scales even between mercy and punishment.

Nymph of Ogygia

Calypso

An island out of time, and an offer of forever — she keeps Odysseus seven years, and means every word of why she wants him to stay.

Enchantress of Aeaea

Circe

She turns men to swine for sport, then warns Odysseus of every danger still ahead — a goddess who plays by rules only she understands.

Portraits replaced with hand-drawn seals — swap in your own artwork any time by editing the .seal blocks in characters.html.
